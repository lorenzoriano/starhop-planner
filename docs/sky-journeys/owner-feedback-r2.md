## The central finding: a journey must be a story, and every card must belong to it

> "I like the premise of left, but I don't think the cards are actually reflecting that premise. Yes, they are at
> increasing distances, but then there is no thread between them. You take any five objects in the sky and you sort
> them by their distance, and then you have five objects of increasing distance. I don't see a real connection between
> them … It just doesn't feel too strong as a connection or as a journey."

> "Again, I like the individual cards on the left, but they don't seem to follow a story, while the ones on the right do."

> "the cards for ν¹ & ν² Boötis they read exactly the same, but they were added for different contexts here, right? On
> the left side, we're talking about the distance of the star, well, on the right side, we're talking about double
> stars, so cards should reflect those differences. … why we're seeing an item for one journey or the other is
> important. … We're looking at it because it's a double. We're looking at it because it's distant, and that
> information has to be in the card. Cards should be kind of personalized to the journey."

> "the jewel box appears in both journeys … on the left side, it makes more sense, and on the right side, it makes less
> sense. … just the card being equal for all the journeys doesn't adapt and doesn't tell me why it's actually there."

> "I love the idea of the right … Do the cards actually reflect that? Are we showing colors and then telling the
> temperature based on the color? There is a pattern here. You start with a really good premise in the journey, but
> then the cards are disjoint. They're not linked together to form a story, at least not in the text, and they're not
> linked to the journey theme. Kind of like almost randomly taken in place there."

> (Cradle to grave) "I love the idea of left, and I can follow it with steps 1, 2, and 3. I'm not sure that 4 and 5 are
> actually doing what you're describing. It's great to have a cycle. Say we start from a stellar nursery, and then we
> have the clusters, and then we have the stars, and then the stars die. Maybe there is a supernova … showing the whole
> life and death of stars in the sky. Not sure the card is doing it, so please pay particular attention to the
> storyline. … I just want to make sure that the execution of the storyline is correct in the card."

Rules that follow:
1. **Theme lens on every card.** For each theme an object can serve, it has a theme-specific question, a "why it's
   here" that names its role in *this* story, what to look at *for this theme* (its color in a color journey, the split
   in a doubles journey), and an answer that moves the story's thread forward. General facts can be shared; the reason
   for the stop cannot.
2. **An object without a lens for a theme is not used in that theme.**
3. **Each theme has a written storyline with ordered beats**, and the engine places stops in beat order. Sorting by a
   number (distance, age) is not a story; the beats must each add a new idea that needs the previous one (e.g. a
   distance ladder where each rung is a new way of measuring that rests on the one before).
4. **Transitions carry the thread**: they say how this stop advances the theme from the last one, in theme terms.
5. Cradle to grave must really run nursery → newborn cluster → grown family → aging giant → death (planetary nebula or
   supernova remnant), with no step that contradicts the label it is given.

## What the observer can actually notice

> "imagine you're the stargazer with your binoculars. You point your binoculars, aha. There is a variable star, but you
> don't know it. You're just looking at a star. … we should prioritize things that the observer will notice. Things
> like brightness, distance, double stars, and clusters are all things that one can see, but a variable star you can't
> really see. … The information about a star being variable, it's good to have it. I just don't think it should be a
> journey on its own."

> "I really like the left journey. I hope these features are somewhat visible with binoculars."

- **A10 (stars that change) is no longer a journey.** Variability stays as card content.
- **A4 (time machine) is retired as a theme** (lost every pair; it is distance sorting by another name). Light-travel
  time remains a line on cards where it helps.
- Every "what you'll see" claim must be true for the selected binoculars: colors only where the eye can see them,
  doubles only if the pair actually splits, nebulae only if visible from that sky.
